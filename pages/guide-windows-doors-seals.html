Windows, Doors & Exterior Seals

Windows and exterior doors depend on more than one visible bead of sealant. Frames, trim, stationary joints, movable seals, sills, thresholds, sweeps, hardware, finishes, and nearby materials should be reviewed together and documented before small changes become larger property concerns.

What This Guide Covers

Treat each window or exterior door as an opening assembly, not an isolated component.
Use caulk at appropriate stationary joints and weatherstripping at movable components, following product instructions.
Document operation, hardware, seals, finishes, moisture, and surrounding materials without assuming one symptom proves the cause.
Record observations, photographs, maintenance, and follow-up dates in the property maintenance plan.

Review the Complete Opening Assembly

A window or exterior door works with the frame, trim, stationary joints, movable seals, sill or threshold, hardware, finishes, and surrounding materials. Review those connected parts instead of treating every draft, stain, or gap as the same problem.

Use Caulk and Weatherstripping for Different Conditions

Caulk is generally used at stationary cracks and gaps. Weatherstripping is used around movable components such as doors and operable windows. The selected material should suit the location, movement, friction, weather exposure, temperature changes, and expected wear.

A seal should not prevent the unit from opening, closing, locking, or latching as intended. Follow the instructions for the installed assembly and sealing product.

Inspect Stationary Exterior Joints

Gaps or separation at visible joints
Cracked, brittle, missing, or detached sealant
Loss of adhesion along one or both sides of a joint
Open joints where trim and surrounding materials meet
Previous patches that have separated again
Paint or finish failure near the joint
Staining or visible deterioration around the opening

Inspect Weatherstripping, Sweeps, and Thresholds

Weatherstripping that is loose, torn, flattened, missing, or no longer making consistent contact
Door sweeps that are damaged, dragging, or leaving an open gap
Threshold components that are loose, worn, damaged, or holding water
Daylight or a noticeable gap at closure points
A door or operable window that no longer closes or latches consistently

Check Operation and Hardware

Open, close, lock, and latch the unit as designed. Record binding, rubbing, loose hardware, movement, unusual resistance, or inconsistent closure. These observations may show that follow-up is needed, but one symptom alone does not establish the concealed cause.

Review the Materials Around the Opening

Staining, swelling, softening, cracking, peeling, or finish deterioration
Changes at interior trim, exterior trim, siding interfaces, or nearby wall finishes
Moisture or condensation that repeatedly appears at the same location
Recurring dampness or unusual odors
Conditions below the opening that may show water has moved beyond the visible joint

Separate Moisture Observation From Source Diagnosis

Moisture may result from water entry, seepage, or condensation on a cold surface. Moisture on glass or nearby finishes does not by itself identify the source. Record when it appears, the weather and indoor conditions, the exact location, and whether nearby materials remain damp or show deterioration.

Do Not Treat Every Draft as a Window-Only Problem

Drafts may be noticeable near windows and doors while other air leakage can be concealed elsewhere in the building. Document the location and conditions without assuming the nearby opening is the only source. Air sealing and planned ventilation should also be considered as separate concerns.

Include Accessible Nearby Joints and Penetrations

Review accessible joints and penetrations near the opening where trim, cladding, utility components, vents, fixtures, or other materials meet the wall. Keep the review focused on visible conditions and document anything that needs a more complete inspection.

Build a Repeatable Maintenance Record

Property location and opening identifier
Manufacturer, product, model, and installation information when available
Photographs of the complete opening and close-up conditions
Observation date, weather, and relevant indoor conditions
Operation, hardware, seal, finish, and surrounding-material observations
Maintenance or repair performed
Product and material instructions used
Unresolved concerns and next follow-up date

When a More Complete Inspection Is Appropriate

Seek a more complete assessment when water entry is active, visible materials are deteriorating, the unit no longer operates securely, previous repairs have not corrected the condition, or the source and pathway are uncertain.
