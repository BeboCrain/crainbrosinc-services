Discuss a Bathtub Leak

Where Bathtub Leaks Start and Where the Water Hides

Bathtub leaks start at the overflow drain, the tub drain, the faucet, or the drain connection. An overflow drain leak comes from the overflow gasket or the overflow pipe behind the tub. A tub drain leak comes from the drain flange or the drain shoe under the tub. A faucet leak drips at the faucet or the water supply connection and runs down the tub surround. A slow leak under the tub can drip for weeks before anyone sees a sign. Water dripping under the tub soaks the subfloor a little at a time.

Water from a bathtub leak sits under the tub out of sight. It gets under the tub and into the subfloor. It drips through to the ceiling below. It spreads across the tile floor and under the baseboards into the rooms beside the bathroom. Tub overflow floods the bathroom floor in minutes, when the tub fills past the overflow drain or the drain cannot carry the water away. Nobody sees a slow leak. The floor softens or the ceiling below stains, and that is how the leak shows. The tile, the subfloor, and the ceiling show the damage where the water went, right there in the bathroom.

Stopping a Bathtub Leak at the Source

Stopping the bathtub leak comes first. Emergency response shuts off the water at the main so the leak stops. Plumbing repairs fix the failed overflow drain, tub drain, faucet, or drain connection and stop the water at the source. The repair depends on which part failed. The overflow drain and the tub drain get checked before the tub goes back into service.

Tracing Bathtub Leak Water Under the Tub, Through the Subfloor, and to the Ceiling Below

To find how far the water traveled, we trace the water under the tub, through the subfloor, and to the ceiling below. Leak detection takes moisture readings through the bathroom floor, the subfloor under the tub, and the ceiling below. Readings also cover the wall framing, the tub surround walls, and the floor framing under the bathroom. During the building inspection, we check the subfloor, the floor joists, the ceiling joists, and the rooms below the bathroom. We open the floor and the ceiling below where the readings call for it. What we find sets the scope of the work. Materials that can dry stay. Materials that cannot dry in reasonable time come out. We check the water at the nearby electrical outlets and the ceiling fixtures below before the area goes back into service.

Cleaning the Bathroom After a Bathtub Leak

Cleaning comes after removal and before drying. We clean the tub, the tile floor, and the remaining subfloor with water and mild detergent. We scrub the tile and the tub surround where the water left residue. The wall framing and the ceiling below that stay get cleaned. The vanity cabinet and the countertop beside the tub get cleaned. Materials that cannot be cleaned come out. Mold on hard surfaces comes off with a scrub brush, detergent, and water. Porous materials that hold mold come out. No paint goes over a surface that is still moldy. Then the bathroom is ready to dry.

Drying the Subfloor, the Tile Floor, and the Ceiling Below After a Bathtub Leak

The subfloor, the tile, and the ceiling below are wet. They need drying before repairs begin. We dry the subfloor under the tub, the tile floor, the ceiling below, and the materials in the rooms the water reached. We run professional drying equipment and take moisture readings until the readings show the structure is dry. We record the readings at every stage until the wood, the subfloor, and the drywall are dry. Damage continues inside materials long after the surface looks dry. Mold grows when wet materials stay wet. The readings show when drying is done.

Frequently Asked Questions About Bathtub Leak Repairs and Restoration

What should I do first after discovering a bathtub leak?

If it is safe, shut off the water to the tub. Stop using the tub and the shower. Keep people away from wet outlets and from the ceiling below. Document the visible conditions. Then call for help stopping the leak, removing the water, and inspecting the damage.

Where does water from a bathtub leak hide?

Water sits under the tub out of sight. It soaks the subfloor under the tub and the floor framing. It drips through to the ceiling below. It spreads across the tile floor and into the rooms beside the bathroom. What you see on the surface is rarely all of it.

What gets cleaned after a bathtub leak?

The tub, the tile floor, the remaining subfloor, the wall framing, and the ceiling below get cleaned with water and mild detergent. The vanity cabinet and the countertop beside the tub get cleaned too. Materials that cannot be cleaned come out. Mold on hard surfaces comes off with a scrub brush, detergent, and water. Porous materials that hold mold come out. What gets cleaned depends on what the inspection finds: the material, the exposure, the physical condition, and the moisture readings.

Can a slow drip under the tub cause real damage?

Yes. A slow leak under the tub can drip for weeks before anyone sees a sign. The water soaks the subfloor a little at a time. The floor softens and the ceiling below stains. Mold grows when the wet materials stay wet.

Can Crain Bros Inc repair the leak and the subfloor, tile floor, and ceiling?

Yes. Crain Bros Inc repairs the failed overflow drain, tub drain, faucet, or drain connection. Then we dry the structure and repair the tub, the subfloor, the tile floor, the ceiling below, the trim, and the paint. The bathroom goes back together around a tub that holds water again.

Does Crain Bros Inc document the damage and the completed work?

Yes. Crain Bros Inc documents the visible conditions, the moisture readings, the approved work, the progress, and the completed repairs. The property owner gets all of it.
