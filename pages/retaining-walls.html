Reclaim Difficult Ground and Create Outdoor Space You Can Use

A steep, eroding, or uneven yard can limit where a property owner can walk, landscape, entertain, garden, or build an outdoor living area. A retaining-wall system can divide difficult ground into organized elevations that support the soil and create functional space.

The wall can become part of the landscape by forming garden terraces, patios, seating areas, fire-pit spaces, walkways, stairs, landings, and defined transitions between different parts of the property.

The completed system should do more than hold soil. The wall, drainage, grading, access, landscaping, and finished outdoor area should work together around how the property owner wants to use the land.

The following photographs show the actual progression of a Crain Bros Inc retaining-wall project. The sequence is provided as a property-owner guide to the visible stages of the work, not as a universal construction specification.

Actual excavation depth, base preparation, drainage, wall components, reinforcement, backfill, and finished details depend on the selected wall system and the conditions at the property.

The purpose of this early work is to establish the planned wall route, manage changing elevations, create construction access, and prepare a dependable supporting path before the first wall course is installed.

Repair or Reconstruct an Existing Retaining Wall

New retaining-wall systems and functional outdoor spaces are the primary focus of this service. Crain Bros Inc also evaluates existing walls that lean, bulge, crack, separate, erode, or no longer serve the property.

Repair may be appropriate when an existing wall can remain serviceable after contributing conditions are corrected. Reconstruction may be required when movement, deterioration, drainage, support, materials, or the required property function cannot be addressed through a limited repair.

Repairing only the visible wall without addressing trapped water, soil movement, erosion, failed drainage, inadequate support, or changes in the retained area can leave the underlying property problem in place.

A reconstruction project can reconsider wall location, stairs, terraces, drainage, access, outdoor use, and the surrounding landscape instead of simply reproducing the previous wall.

Leaning, Bulging or Displaced Walls

Cracking and Separation

Consider crack locations, displaced wall units, failed joints, separated caps, stairs, wall ends, and connections to nearby walks or structures.

Blocked or Failed Drainage

Trace where water collects, whether drainage components remain functional, and where collected water should leave the retained area.

Evaluate erosion channels, undermined areas, exposed components, upper slopes, lower grades, wall ends, and locations where runoff continues moving soil.

Repair versus Reconstruction

Develop the scope around the wall condition, materials, support, drainage, access, retained ground, intended property use, and whether the existing system can remain serviceable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are signs that a retaining wall should be inspected?

Leaning, bulging, cracking, separation, displaced units, failed caps, settlement, erosion, soil loss, blocked drainage, water collecting behind the wall, and changes at nearby improvements are reasons to investigate.

Why does water affect a retaining wall?

Retained soil can hold water. Collected water increases pressure behind the wall and may contribute to movement, cracking, displacement, erosion, deterioration, or failure.

Can a leaning retaining wall be repaired?

That depends on wall condition, movement, materials, support, retained soil, drainage, nearby loads, access, and whether the cause can be corrected while the existing wall remains serviceable.

Does every retaining wall use the same construction method?

No. Height, dimensions, layout, material, soil, drainage, loads, reinforcement requirements, nearby structures, utilities, access, and the selected wall system affect construction.

Why is the first course important?

The first course establishes wall location, alignment, curvature, and initial elevations. The supporting base must be prepared so later courses do not follow an unstable or inaccurate foundation.

Do retaining walls need drainage?

Retaining-wall systems generally need a method for managing water in retained soil and a workable outlet that allows collected water to leave the system.

What is placed behind a retaining wall?

Materials depend on the selected wall system and property conditions. Work may include drainage aggregate, drainage piping, reinforcement when required, and controlled backfill.

Can a retaining wall create usable property space?

A wall may organize a slope into defined elevations for access, landscaping, walkways, stairs, outdoor areas, or maintenance while supporting soil and coordinating drainage.

Can stairs be integrated into a retaining wall?

Stairs may be coordinated with wall construction, drainage, approach areas, walking surfaces, surrounding grade, and adjacent improvements.

Should an old wall be rebuilt in the same location?

Not automatically. Wall function, soil, drainage, erosion, access, utilities, boundaries, nearby improvements, and the cause of prior movement should be evaluated first.

What should property owners monitor after the work?

Watch for new movement, cracking, settlement, soil loss, blocked outlets, erosion, changing drainage paths, stair separation, and water collecting above or behind the wall.

How does Crain Bros Inc approach a retaining-wall project?

Crain Bros Inc investigates the wall together with soil, drainage, water paths, grading, nearby improvements, access, and the required property function before developing the work scope.
