What Drywall Covers

Finishing is where drywall is won or lost. Joint treatment, sanding, and finish level determine what the wall looks like under real light, and a poorly finished wall shows every joint once paint and side lighting are on it.

Texture matching is the hard part in repair work. Existing texture was applied by a particular hand with particular material, and matching it into a patch is the difference between a repair nobody notices and one everybody does.

In restoration, drywall is also a moisture question. Board that has been wet is removed rather than dried in place, and the cut line is set by moisture readings rather than by convenience.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do drywall repairs sometimes show?

Usually texture matching or finish level. Existing texture was applied by a specific hand with specific material, and side lighting reveals any difference in the surface.

Can wet drywall be dried instead of replaced?

Generally it is replaced. Board that has been saturated loses integrity and can hold moisture against framing, and the cut line is set by moisture readings rather than by where a cut would be convenient.

What is a finish level?

How completely joints and fasteners are treated and sanded. Higher levels matter most in rooms with strong side lighting or gloss paint, where every imperfection is visible.

Do you match ceiling texture?

We match as closely as the texture type allows. Some ceiling textures are genuinely difficult to match into, and we will say so before starting rather than after.

Is drywall part of restoration work?

Yes. It is usually among the first materials removed and among the last replaced, because what is behind it has to be dry and verified first.

Do you self-perform drywall?

Yes. Hanging, finishing, and texture are performed by our own crews.
