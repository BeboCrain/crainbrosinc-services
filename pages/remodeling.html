Remodeling an Existing Home or Commercial Building

Existing buildings contain conditions that are not always visible before work begins. Previous alterations, concealed utilities, damaged framing, uneven floors, moisture, outdated systems and incomplete drawings can affect the final scope. Building inspection services can document accessible conditions before remodeling begins, while building inspection and diagnostics can help investigate conditions that require deeper analysis.

A complete remodeling plan connects the room layout to the structure and building systems around it. Removing a wall may require structural framing work, relocation of electrical wiring, changes to plumbing, or adjustments to heating, cooling and ventilation. Planning those connections before finish work begins helps reduce avoidable rework.

Crain Bros Inc provides and coordinates the services included in the approved remodeling scope. Larger projects may also use our general contracting services for project planning, scheduling, supervision, trade coordination and closeout.

Selective Demolition, Preparation and Concealed Conditions

Remodeling often begins by protecting areas that will remain and selectively removing cabinets, counters, fixtures, flooring, walls, ceilings, trim or other materials included in the scope. Access paths, contents, occupied areas and debris movement should be considered before demolition begins.

Opening an existing assembly may reveal utilities, deterioration, moisture damage, inadequate framing or previous work that was not visible during the initial review. When a concealed condition is found, Crain Bros Inc documents the condition, explains how it affects the planned work and develops the additional correction required before it is performed. Hidden correction is not represented as automatically included when it was not part of the approved scope.

Renovation work that disturbs painted surfaces in an applicable pre-1978 home or child-occupied facility may involve lead-safe requirements. Property owners can review the EPA Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting Program and the federal residential property renovation requirements when the planned demolition, wall or ceiling access, window or door work, trim removal, surface preparation or other remodeling activity may disturb painted materials.

  • Floor and finish protection
  • Work-zone and access-path preparation
  • Cabinet, countertop, fixture and appliance removal
  • Selective wall, ceiling and flooring demolition
  • Detach-and-reset work included in the scope
  • Debris handling and construction cleanup
  • Inspection of exposed assemblies
  • Substrate and rough-opening preparation

Frequently Asked Questions About Remodeling

Does Crain Bros Inc remodel both homes and commercial properties?
