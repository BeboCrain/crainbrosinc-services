Working Inside an Existing Building

New construction starts from nothing and everything is known. Remodeling starts from whatever the last person built, which is rarely what the drawings said and occasionally not what code allows. Opening a wall is the point where a remodel becomes real.

That is why remodeling favors a contractor who self-performs. When framing, plumbing, electrical, and finish trades are all in-house, discovering something unexpected behind a wall is a scheduling conversation rather than a renegotiation across four companies.

It also means one standard of finish. Trim, cabinetry, countertops, and paint are the parts of a project people actually touch and look at every day, and they are the parts that suffer most when a job is split across whoever was available.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between remodeling and general contracting?

The same trades applied to different work. Remodeling changes and upgrades a building that already exists. General contracting covers new construction, additions, and full rebuilds.

Do you subcontract remodel work?

No. We self-perform cabinetry, countertops, trim carpentry, flooring, painting, drywall, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, roofing, siding, decks, masonry, concrete, insulation, and windows and doors.

What happens if you find something unexpected behind a wall?

It is common in older structures, and it is one of the reasons self-performing matters. Because the trades are in-house, an unexpected discovery is a scheduling conversation rather than a renegotiation across several companies.

Do you build custom cabinets?

Yes. Cabinets are fabricated and installed to the space rather than fitted from stock sizes.

Can you remodel a commercial space?

Yes. We work on residential and commercial property throughout Northeast Arkansas.

Do you handle code requirements on a remodel?

Yes. Code review is part of scoping the work, which matters more in remodeling than in new construction because existing conditions are frequently not compliant.
