What Gutters Covers

A roof collects a considerable volume of water and concentrates it at the edges. Gutters carry it, but downspouts and discharge decide whether it leaves the building or soaks into the soil sitting against the foundation.

That soil is the reason gutters matter structurally. Soil that repeatedly saturates and dries expands and contracts, and a foundation sitting on it records that movement as cracking and settling. Discharging roof water next to a foundation is one of the most common causes of foundation movement we see.

Sizing matters too. An undersized system overflows in the rain that actually matters, which is exactly when overflow does damage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do gutters really affect a foundation?

Yes, and it is one of the most common causes of foundation movement. Roof water discharged next to a foundation saturates the soil, and soil that repeatedly saturates and dries moves. The structure sitting on it records that movement.

How far should downspouts discharge from the house?

Far enough that the water does not return to the foundation. The right distance depends on grade and soil, which is why discharge is designed rather than defaulted.

Why do gutters overflow even when they are clean?

Usually because the system is undersized or improperly pitched for the roof area feeding it. It handles ordinary rain and fails in heavy rain, which is when overflow does damage.

Are gutter guards worth it?

They help where debris accumulation is a recurring problem. They are not a substitute for correct sizing and pitch.

Do you repair fascia when replacing gutters?

Yes. Fascia damaged by overflow or improper hanging is repaired before new gutters go up, because hanging new gutters on failed fascia repeats the failure.

Can downspouts tie into underground drainage?

Yes. Where surface discharge is not moving water far enough, downspouts can be tied into subsurface drainage.
