Step-by-Step Water Damage Restoration Process

1. Emergency Conditions

Respond to the water event, review access and safety conditions, extract standing or absorbed water as part of property damage mitigation, and manipulate or protect personal property and contents when needed to reduce additional damage.

2. Source Review

Look for the leak, water entry point, equipment condition, plumbing failure, roof condition, or other source that allowed water into the structure.

3. Inspection and Cause and Origin Determination

Determine what happened, where the water originated, how it traveled, and which materials, building assemblies, systems, and contents were affected.

4. Assessment and Diagnosis

Assess affected building materials, moisture conditions, concealed areas, structural concerns, building systems, and contents to diagnose the scope of the restoration project.

5. Restoration Design

Design the work sequence around the property conditions, client needs, contents handling, containment, material removal, cleaning, drying, repair, and reconstruction requirements.

6. Contents and Personal Property Care

Manipulate, protect, inventory, clean, deodorize, pack out, store, or separate affected personal property and contents according to the needs of the property and client.

7. Containment

Separate affected work areas from unaffected spaces and use controlled pressure relationships where appropriate to help contain dust, moisture, debris, and affected air within the work zone.

8. Removal of Unsalvageable Materials

Remove building materials that cannot be restored, cleaned, or dried effectively while protecting materials and assemblies that can remain.

9. Cleaning and Remediation

Clean and remediate affected areas with the goal of bringing remaining building materials to a sanitary condition before drying systems are applied.

10. Drying

Apply air movement and dehumidification according to the affected materials, moisture readings, psychrometric conditions, containment configuration, and restoration design.

11. Repair Planning

Define what must be repaired, replaced, rebuilt, or finished after cleaning, remediation, and drying are complete.

12. Reconstruction

Reconstruct damaged drywall, ceilings, flooring, trim, cabinets, framing areas, wall assemblies, and finishes according to the restoration design and repair plan.

13. Final Documentation

Document completed work, final property conditions, repair progress, restoration decisions, and the completed condition of the project.

Emergency Water Damage Services from the First Response Through Reconstruction

When a leak, flood, condensation problem, roof leak, plumbing failure, water heater leak, HVAC drain issue, or hidden wall cavity moisture problem affects a property, the visible wet area is only part of the story. Water can travel through ceiling cavities, walls, floors, insulation, trim, cabinets, framing, electrical areas, mechanical spaces, and contents before the damage is fully understood.

Crain Bros Inc approaches water damage as a full service restoration process. That includes emergency service needs after a water event occurs to the property, leak detection, cause and origin determination, assessment, diagnosis, restoration design, structural drying, moisture documentation, cleaning and remediation, personal property and contents care, repair planning, rebuilding, reconstruction, and professional documentation throughout the project.

We also work on properties that are involved in insurance claims. Our role is to inspect, document, plan, perform, and report the restoration work as a contractor. We document damage, property conditions, project scope, work progress, and completed repairs so property owners have clear information they can use on their own behalf to file insurance claims.

Water Sources and Initial Damage

Water damage can begin with water heater leaks, flooded basements, roof leaks, plumbing leaks, drain leaks, ceiling damage, or water entering concealed wall and electrical assemblies.

Plumbing Leaks

Plumbing leaks can involve water heaters, supply and drain piping, plumbing connections, floor decking, wall assemblies, finishes, and concealed areas. Inspection and cause and origin determination help identify where the leak began, how the water traveled, and which materials require mitigation, cleaning, drying, repair, or reconstruction.

HVAC Leaks

HVAC leaks can involve condensation drains, equipment conditions, mechanical spaces, and nearby building materials. Inspection helps determine where the water originated, how it traveled, and what needs to be dried, cleaned, repaired, or reconstructed.

Containment Systems During Water Damage Restoration

Containment is used when affected areas need to be separated from unaffected spaces. A contained work area can be placed under negative pressure in relation to surrounding parts of the building so dust, moisture, debris, and potentially affected air stay inside the damaged zone instead of spreading into livable or unaffected spaces.

Keeping the affected area controlled can also make cleanup more efficient and support drying performance by focusing air movement and dehumidification inside the work zone. This helps the restoration process fit the client's living or business needs while the damaged portion of the property is being cleaned, dried, repaired, or reconstructed.

Moisture Documentation and Psychrometrics

Moisture documentation tracks what building materials are wet and how those materials respond over time. Psychrometric readings track air conditions, helping determine how the drying environment is performing and whether equipment is actually removing moisture from building materials.

This is why structural drying should not be judged only by how a surface looks or how long equipment has been running. The process should be based on the materials involved, the air conditions, the readings, and the restoration goal.

Repair and Reconstruction After Water Damage

After the source is addressed and affected materials are cleaned, dried, removed, or prepared, repair and reconstruction can restore damaged drywall, finishes, trim, and surrounding assemblies.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do first after discovering water damage?

Stop the water source if you safely can, and shut off electricity to affected areas if water is near outlets or fixtures. Then call us at (870) 935-6019. Take photos before moving anything if it is safe to do so.

Do you help determine what caused the water damage?

Yes. We inspect the affected areas, look for the source or origin of the water, document what we find, and use that information to plan the restoration work.

Do you handle emergency water extraction?

Yes. Water extraction may be the first step when standing or absorbed water needs to be removed before drying and repair work can move forward.

Can water damage be hidden behind walls or under floors?

Yes. Water often travels farther than the visible wet area. Wall cavities, insulation, floor decking, trim, cabinets, ceilings, and mechanical spaces may need inspection.

Do you dry the structure before rebuilding?

Yes. Repairing over wet materials can create future problems. The drying process should be based on the actual materials and moisture conditions involved.

Do you work on properties involved in insurance claims?

Yes. We work on properties involved in insurance claims as a contractor. We document damage, property conditions, project scope, work progress, and completed repairs so property owners have clear information they can use on their own behalf.
