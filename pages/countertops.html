What Countertops Covers

Templating happens after cabinets are set, because the countertop has to match the installed reality rather than the plan. Walls that are not straight, cabinets that are not perfectly aligned, and openings that shifted during construction all get accounted for at template.

Material choice drives more than appearance. Seam placement, overhang support, cutout locations, and edge treatment all depend on what the material can do, and those decisions are made before fabrication rather than discovered at installation.

Support is the detail most often underestimated. Overhangs beyond what the material can carry unsupported need bracing designed in, not added after the fact.

Frequently Asked Questions

When are countertops templated?

After the cabinets are installed. The countertop has to match what was actually built, including walls that are not straight and cabinets that shifted slightly during installation.

Can seams be avoided entirely?

Not always. Material comes in finite sizes, and large or complex runs will have seams. What can be controlled is where they fall and how visible they are.

Does overhang need support?

Beyond a certain distance, yes, and it depends on the material. Support is designed in before fabrication rather than added afterward.

Are cutouts made from the fixture or the spec sheet?

From the actual fixture wherever possible. Spec sheets and delivered products do not always agree, and a cutout is not adjustable after the fact.

How long between templating and installation?

It depends on the material and fabrication schedule. That timeline is established at template so the rest of the project can be sequenced around it.

Do you self-perform countertops?

Yes. Templating, fabrication, and installation are performed by our own crews.
