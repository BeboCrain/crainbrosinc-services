Storm Damage Restoration

Storm damage is envelope damage. Something opened the building to the weather, and everything that follows depends on how quickly that opening is closed and how completely the damage is found.

The Damage You Can See From the Ground Is Not the Scope

Wind and hail damage is routinely underestimated because most of it happens where nobody looks. Lifted shingles, fractured decking, damaged flashing, bruised roofing material, and compromised fasteners are not visible from a driveway, and a roof can be genuinely damaged while still looking intact.

That matters because storm damage keeps working. A roof that is compromised but not obviously broken lets water in slowly, and the interior damage shows up weeks later as staining, then as mold, long after the storm has been forgotten.

The first job is closing the envelope. The second is establishing the real extent of what was damaged, on the roof and everywhere the weather reached.

Frequently Asked Questions

My roof looks fine from the ground. Could it still be damaged?

Yes, and this is common. Lifted shingles, fractured decking, damaged flashing, and compromised fasteners are not visible from a driveway. A roof can be genuinely damaged while looking intact.

How quickly should storm damage be looked at?

Quickly. A compromised roof lets water in slowly, and interior damage often appears weeks later as staining and then as mold, long after the storm.

Do you tarp before repairing?

Where the building is open, yes. Tarping is temporary weather protection that stops further loss while the permanent repair is scoped and scheduled.

Does storm damage always mean water damage?

Not always, but often. If the envelope was open for any length of time, water entered, and that is handled with extraction and monitored drying rather than assumed away.

Do you handle both roof and interior repairs?

Yes. We self-perform roofing along with the interior trades, so one company carries the project from the envelope through the finished interior.

What documentation do you provide?

We document the damage, the property conditions, and the project work. That documentation goes to the property owner so they can file their claim more effectively on their own behalf.
