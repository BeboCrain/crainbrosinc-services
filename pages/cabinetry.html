What Cabinetry Covers

The practical difference shows up at the edges. Fillers, dead corners, and awkward gaps are what happen when fixed sizes meet a real room, and they are the compromises people live with for twenty years.

Custom work also means the design responds to how the space is used rather than to what sizes were available. Cabinet depth, drawer configuration, and storage type get decided for the room instead of selected from a list.

Installation matters as much as fabrication. Cabinets set on out-of-level floors against out-of-plumb walls require shimming and scribing, and doors and drawers reveal immediately when that was skipped.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between custom and stock cabinetry?

Custom is built to the room. Stock is built to fixed sizes and the room accommodates it with fillers and dead corners. The difference shows up at the edges, and you live with it for a long time.

Can existing cabinets be refinished instead of replaced?

Yes, where the boxes are structurally sound and the layout still works. Refinishing solves a finish problem. It does not solve a layout problem.

Do you design the kitchen layout?

Yes. Layout, cabinet configuration, and storage type are decided for how you use the space rather than selected from available sizes.

How long does custom cabinetry take?

Longer than ordering stock, because it is fabricated for your room. That timeline is established up front and coordinated with the rest of the project schedule.

Do you install cabinetry you did not build?

Case by case. Installation quality depends partly on how the cabinets were built, so we look at what is being installed first.

Do you self-perform cabinetry?

Yes. Fabrication and installation are both performed by our own crews.
