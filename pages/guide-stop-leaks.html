Part 1

Stopping Leaks Before They Damage Your Home

Small leaks can affect cabinets, flooring, walls, ceilings, and other materials before the source is obvious. A written maintenance plan starts by identifying water-connected equipment, recording what is installed, and scheduling inspections and follow-up before aging components fail.

What This Guide Covers

Record the equipment, fixtures, supply connections, and drain connections installed at the property.
Inspect accessible components and the materials around them for developing conditions.
Use instructions for the exact installed product instead of one universal replacement schedule.
Document inspection, service, repair, and replacement dates in a property-specific maintenance record.

Identify Every Potential Leak Source

A maintenance plan is more useful when it identifies the actual products and water-connected systems at the property. Record the manufacturer, model, serial number, installation date, warranty, service history, and available maintenance instructions for major appliances and water-connected equipment.

Keep the information with inspection notes, photographs, repair records, and future follow-up dates. This creates a property record that can be updated when equipment is serviced or replaced.

Inspect Water Heaters for Leaks

Review accessible areas around the water heater and its visible connections. Record moisture, corrosion, unusual sounds, changes in performance, rusty hot water, and conditions around the drain pan or its discharge path.

A maintenance record should identify when the equipment was installed, what service has been performed, and when the next inspection or manufacturer-recommended action is due.

Moisture around the unit or visible connections
Corrosion at fittings or accessible components
Unusual sounds or changes in performance
Rusty hot water
Conditions around the drain pan and discharge path

Inspect Washing-Machine Hoses for Leaks

Inspect accessible hot and cold inlet hoses and fittings. Record the hose installation date and follow the replacement and maintenance guidance for the installed product.

Bulges, cuts, kinks, or visible wear
Corrosion or dampness at fittings
Active dripping or leakage
Missing installation or replacement-date information

Inspect Refrigerator and Ice-Maker Lines for Leaks

Inspect accessible ice-maker and water-dispenser lines, fittings, and shutoff valves. Also review the nearby flooring and wall materials because a slow leak may first appear as staining, swelling, corrosion, or recurring dampness.

Inspect Dishwasher Connections for Leaks

Check accessible supply and drain connections, nearby cabinets, flooring, the door seal, and surrounding materials. Document moisture, staining, swelling, deterioration, or unusual odors rather than waiting for water to spread beyond the appliance area.

Inspect Under-Sink Components for Leaks

Areas below sinks can contain several possible leak locations in a small space. Inspect accessible faucet supplies, shutoff valves, drains, disposals, filters, the cabinet floor, and wall penetrations.

Corrosion or dampness at supplies and valves
Staining or swelling on the cabinet floor
Softened or deteriorated material
Recurring odors
Moisture around drains, disposals, filters, or penetrations

Inspect Toilets, Tubs, and Showers for Leaks

Review wet areas for fixture movement, moisture, failed sealant, soft flooring, staining below the area, and water escaping outside the intended surface or enclosure.

Record the exact location and visible condition. A symptom may show that follow-up is needed without establishing the complete concealed source or pathway.

Follow Product Guidance to Prevent Leaks

Do not apply one inspection or replacement schedule to every appliance or connection. Use the manual and manufacturer guidance for the exact product installed, then record the action and date in the property maintenance plan.

Maintenance record

Record every inspection, service, repair, and replacement date so future work is based on the property and products actually installed.

Plan Leak Repairs and Replacements Before Failure

Planning becomes more important as equipment ages, repairs become frequent, corrosion develops, leakage appears, performance declines, or a failure could release water into the property.
