What Plumbing Covers

Supply lines fail, drains back up, and water heaters reach end of life. When any of those happen inside a finished building, the plumbing repair is the small part and the water damage is the large part.

Remodels concentrate the risk. Moving a kitchen or bath means opening walls and floors and relocating supply and drain lines, and drain slope is not negotiable. A drain that runs level enough to fit the framing is a drain that will need to be opened again.

We self-perform plumbing and restoration both, so a failure that becomes a water damage project does not require two companies to sort out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle both the plumbing failure and the water damage?

Yes. We self-perform plumbing and restoration, so a supply line failure that became a water damage project is one company rather than two.

Can plumbing be moved in a remodel?

Yes, within the limits of drain slope and venting. Supply lines are flexible about routing. Drains are not, and a drain forced to fit the framing rather than the slope requirement will need to be opened again.

What is a repipe and when is it needed?

Replacing supply piping throughout a structure. It is appropriate where the existing material has reached end of life and repeated repairs are treating the symptom.

Why does drain venting matter?

Without proper venting, drains do not flow correctly and traps can siphon, which lets sewer gas into the building. Venting is not optional and it is frequently wrong in older work.

Should a water heater be relocated?

Sometimes. A water heater in a finished space above living area is a water damage event waiting for a schedule. Relocation during a remodel is far cheaper than the repair afterward.

Do you do plumbing on commercial work?

Yes. We work on residential and commercial property throughout Northeast Arkansas.
