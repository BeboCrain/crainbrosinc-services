Discuss a Sink Leak

Where Sink Leaks Start and Where the Water Hides

Sink leaks start at the faucet, the water supply line, the water valve, the sink drain, or the garbage disposal. A faucet leak drips at the base of the faucet or down the back of the sink. A water supply line can fail at the connection or at the water valve under the sink. A sink drain can leak at the trap or at the drain connection. A garbage disposal leak starts at the disposal housing or at the drain connection. A slow leak under the sink can drip for weeks before anyone sees a sign. Water dripping under the sink soaks the cabinet base a little at a time.

Water under the sink hides inside the cabinet. The water sits in the cabinet base. It soaks the cabinet bottom and the wall behind the cabinet. It runs down to the floor under the cabinet. Water under the kitchen sink spreads across the kitchen floor. Water under the bathroom sink spreads across the bathroom floor. The water can wick up the wall above the sink. Nobody sees a slow leak until the cabinet floor swells or the floor buckles. Wet cabinets and a wet floor are often how the leak gets noticed. Cabinet damage, floor damage, and wall damage follow the water wherever it travels.

Stopping a Sink Leak at the Source

Stopping the leak comes first. Emergency response shuts off the water at the water valve before the water spreads further. Plumbing repairs fix the failed faucet, water supply line, sink drain, or garbage disposal and stop the water at the source. The failed part sets the repair. The water valve under the sink is checked before the area goes back into service.

Tracing Sink Leak Water Through Cabinets, Walls, and Floors

Finding how far the water traveled means tracing the water through the cabinet, the wall, and the floor. Leak detection takes moisture readings through the cabinet floor and the wall behind the cabinet. Readings also cover the wall framing, the kitchen floor, and the bathroom floor. During the building inspection, we check the bathroom vanity cabinet, the kitchen cabinets, the sink drain connections, and the rooms beside the kitchen or bathroom. We open the cabinet base and the wall behind the cabinet where the readings call for it. What we find tells us what the work needs. Materials that can dry stay. Materials that cannot dry in reasonable time come out. Water at the disposal connection and the nearby electrical outlets is checked before the area goes back into service.

Drying Wet Cabinets, Walls, and Floors After a Sink Leak

Wet cabinets and wet floors need drying before repairs begin. Drying covers the cabinet base, the wall cavity behind the cabinet, the floor under the cabinet, and the materials in the rooms the water reached. We run professional drying equipment to verified moisture readings. We record the readings at every stage until the wood, the cabinets, and the drywall are dry. Damage continues inside materials long after the surface looks dry. Mold in the cabinet, mold under the sink, and mold behind the cabinet grow when wet materials stay wet. The readings decide when drying is done.

Frequently Asked Questions About Sink Leak Repairs and Restoration

What should I do first after discovering a sink leak?

If it is safe, shut off the water supply valve under the sink. Stop using the faucet and the garbage disposal. Keep people away from wet outlets. Document the visible conditions. Then request help with source control, water removal, and inspection.

Where does water from a sink leak hide?

Water hides in the cabinet base. It soaks the wall behind the cabinet and the floor under the cabinet. It wicks up the wall above the sink. It spreads across the kitchen floor or the bathroom floor and into the next room. What shows on the surface is rarely the full affected area.

Can a slow drip under the sink cause real damage?

Yes. A slow leak under the sink can drip for weeks before anyone sees a sign. The water soaks the cabinet base a little at a time. The cabinet floor swells and the floor buckles. Mold grows in the cabinet, under the sink, and behind the cabinet when the wet materials stay wet.

How do you decide which materials can dry and which must come out?

The decision follows what the inspection finds: the material, the exposure, the physical condition, and the moisture readings. Materials that can dry stay. Materials that cannot dry in reasonable time come out.

Can Crain Bros Inc repair the leak and the cabinets, flooring, and drywall?

Yes. Crain Bros Inc repairs the failed faucet, water supply line, valve, sink drain, or garbage disposal. Then Crain Bros Inc dries the structure and repairs the cabinets, the flooring, the drywall, the trim, and the paint. One contractor carries the job from the first call to the finished repair.

Does Crain Bros Inc document the damage and the completed work?

Yes. Crain Bros Inc documents the visible conditions, the moisture readings, the approved work, the progress, and the completed repairs. The property owner gets all of it.
