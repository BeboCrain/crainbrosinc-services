What Trim Carpentry Covers

Finish carpentry is unforgiving because nothing is hidden. Joints, reveals, and returns are visible at eye level in good light, and the difference between careful work and quick work is obvious to anyone in the room.

It is also the trade most affected by everything before it. Trim installed against out-of-plane walls, uneven floors, or racked openings requires scribing and adjustment, and rushing the trades ahead of it shows up here.

In older buildings, matching existing profiles is its own problem. Historic trim profiles are frequently no longer stocked, which means either milling to match or making an honest decision about where new work begins.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you match trim in an older house?

Often, though stock profiles frequently no longer exist for older work. The choice is milling to match or deciding deliberately where new work begins, and that is a conversation before the work rather than after.

Why does trim look wrong in some rooms?

Usually because the surfaces behind it are not square, plumb, or flat. Trim installed without scribing to those conditions telegraphs every one of them.

Is trim carpentry part of a remodel?

Almost always. It is the finish layer, and it is the part of a remodel people actually see and touch.

Do you build built-ins?

Yes. Built-in shelving, benches, and cabinetry integrated into the structure rather than set against it.

Do you hang doors?

Yes, including casing and finishing, and including openings that need to be reframed to fit the door correctly.

Do you self-perform trim carpentry?

Yes. It is one of the trades our own crews perform.
