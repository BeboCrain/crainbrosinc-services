Discuss a Shower Leak

Where Shower Leaks Start and Where the Water Hides

Tile and grout finish the shower wall, and a waterproofing liner or waterproofing compound goes behind the tile to keep water out of the wall. Grout cracks open a path for water behind the tile, and caulk fails where the wall meets the pan and where the fixtures pass through the wall. A shower pan leak starts at a cracked pan or a failed pan liner. A shower faucet leak starts at the faucet or the pipes that lead to the faucet. A shower drain leak starts at the drain assembly or the drain connection under the pan. A slow shower leak can drip behind the tile for weeks before anyone sees a sign.

Water from a shower leak hides behind the tile. The water gets behind the finish and into the wall. It soaks the backer board and the wall framing, then runs down the wall cavity to the subfloor. From there it spreads across the bathroom floor and under the baseboards, and it runs through the floor to the ceiling and the room below. Cracked tile or loose tile means the water is already behind the finish. Tile damage, wall damage, and floor damage show up where the water went, right there in the bathroom.

Stopping a Shower Leak at the Source

Stopping the shower leak comes first. Emergency response shuts off the water at the main so the leak stops. Plumbing repairs fix the failed shower faucet, drain assembly, or supply pipes and stop the water at the source. The failed part sets the repair. The shower stays out of service until the source is fixed and the area is dry.

Tracing Shower Leak Water Through the Tile, Backer Board, and Floor

Finding how far the water traveled means tracing the water through the tile, the backer board, the wall, and the floor. Leak detection takes moisture readings through the tile, the backer board, and the wall framing. Readings also cover the subfloor under the shower, the ceiling below where the bathroom is upstairs, and the crawlspace under the bathroom. During the building inspection, we check the shower pan, the backer board, the framing, the subfloor, and the rooms beside the bathroom. We open the tile and the wall where the readings call for it. What we find tells us what the work needs. Materials that can dry stay. Materials that cannot dry in reasonable time come out. Water at the nearby electrical outlets and switches is checked before the area goes back into service.

Drying the Wall Framing and the Subfloor After a Shower Leak

We remove what must come out, then clean the remaining materials and the area. We dry the wall framing, the wall cavity, and the subfloor to verified readings. We run professional drying equipment to verified moisture readings. We record the readings at every stage until the wood and the drywall are dry. Damage continues inside materials long after the surface looks dry. Mold grows when wet materials stay wet. The readings decide when drying is done.

The finished walls are painted after the drywall is repaired. The bathroom floor gets new flooring where the water damaged it. Frequently Asked Questions About Shower Leak Repairs and Restoration

What should I do first after discovering a shower leak?

If it is safe, shut off the water at the main. Stop using the shower. Keep people away from wet outlets and switches. Document the visible conditions. Then request help with source control and inspection.

Where does water from a shower leak hide?

Water hides behind the tile. It soaks the backer board and the wall framing. It runs down the wall cavity to the subfloor. It spreads under the bathroom floor and through the floor to the ceiling below. What shows on the surface is rarely the full affected area.

Can a slow drip behind the shower tile cause real damage?

Yes. A slow shower leak can drip behind the tile for weeks before anyone sees a sign. The water gets through the waterproofing and soaks the backer board a little at a time. The tile loosens and the grout cracks. Mold grows behind the tile and inside the wall when the wet materials stay wet.

How do you decide which materials can dry and which must come out?

The decision follows what the inspection finds: the material, the exposure, the physical condition, and the moisture readings. Materials that can dry stay. Materials that cannot dry in reasonable time come out.

Can Crain Bros Inc repair the leak and the tile, backer board, and drywall?

Yes. We repair the failed shower faucet, drain assembly, or supply pipes. Then we dry the structure. The shower pan is repaired or replaced first. Then the backer board is replaced and the tile goes back on. The drywall is repaired and the walls are painted. One contractor carries the job from the first call to the finished repair.

Does Crain Bros Inc document the damage and the completed work?

Yes. Crain Bros Inc documents the visible conditions, the moisture readings, the approved work, the progress, and the completed repairs. The property owner gets all of it.
