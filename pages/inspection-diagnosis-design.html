Find the Cause Before Designing the Repair

A stain on a ceiling is not the problem. It is evidence of one. Repairing the stained material without establishing where the water came from produces a repair that fails, and the second repair costs more than the first because the damage kept spreading in between.

This is the part of the work most often skipped, and it is the part that determines whether everything after it is correct. Inspection establishes what is actually there. Diagnosis establishes why. Design establishes what has to be done, in what order, and how the work meets building code requirements.

We document the damage, the property conditions, and the project work. That documentation goes to the property owner so they can file their claim more effectively on their own behalf.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an inspection and a diagnosis?

An inspection records what is there. A diagnosis explains why it is there. You can have a complete inspection and still not know the cause, and a repair built on that will address the symptom while leaving the cause running.

Will you inspect a property you are not going to repair?

Yes. Inspection, diagnosis, and consulting stand on their own. Call (870) 935-6019 to discuss what you need.

Can you review a scope of work someone else prepared?

Yes. Scope review is part of our consulting work, and it is most valuable before the work starts rather than after.

Do you review building code requirements?

Yes. Code requirements shape what a compliant repair looks like, so they are treated as a design input from the beginning.

Do you arrange independent testing?

Yes. Where a project calls for it we coordinate third-party independent laboratory testing, so the results come from a party with no stake in the outcome of the repair.

What does the documentation include?

The damage, the property conditions, and the project work. That documentation goes to the property owner so they can file their claim more effectively on their own behalf.
